Every spring, I make at least two trips to the Dallas Arboretum and I never regret it. When the Dallas Blooms festival is in full swing — hundreds of thousands of tulips, daffodils, and pansies covering the grounds in every color imaginable — it's genuinely breathtaking. This is one of those things that's right in our backyard that we should all appreciate more.

## Dallas Blooms Festival

The annual Dallas Blooms celebration runs February through April and is the largest floral festival in the Southwest. We're talking 100,000+ bulbs blooming across the 66 acres of gardens along White Rock Lake.

## Practical Info

- **Address:** 8525 Garland Rd, Dallas, TX 75218
- **Hours:** Daily 9am–5pm (extended during festivals)
- **Admission:** $20 adults / $17 seniors / $12 children (3–12) / Free under 2
- **Parking:** On-site parking is $15; free shuttle from the White Rock DART station on select days

## What to See

**A-Peeling Garden** — the tulip garden. Stop here first; it's stunning in the morning light.

**Jonsson Color Garden** — the heart of the Arboretum, beautifully landscaped year-round but especially gorgeous during spring bloom.

**Rory Meyers Children's Adventure Garden** — 8 acres just for kids with 150+ interactive exhibits. My niece could spend an entire day in here.

**The DEW Terrace on White Rock Lake** — grab a snack here and just take in the view. On a clear day it's postcard-perfect.

## Tips for Visiting with Kids

- Go early on weekdays — weekends during Dallas Blooms are *busy*
- Wear comfortable shoes, you'll do a lot of walking
- Bring a stroller for little ones — the paths are paved and wide
- Pack snacks but know that the café and restaurant on-site are both solid options
- The splash pad and children's garden will add at least an extra hour to your trip (plan for it, it's worth it)

## Photography Tips

Golden hour — about 30 minutes after the park opens — is magic for photos. The tulips, the lake backdrop, the morning light. Even my phone camera makes me look like I know what I'm doing.

Spring in Dallas is *real* and the Arboretum is proof. Don't let another season go by without going!
